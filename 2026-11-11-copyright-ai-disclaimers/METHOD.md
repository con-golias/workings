# Method

## Source
- **US Copyright Office Public Records System**, through the same API its website uses:
  `api.publicrecords.copyright.gov/search_service_external/`. `simple_search_dsl` (keyword search, 100 records a page)
  finds records; `search_unique_record?query=<public_records_id>` returns the full record, including
  `material_excluded_statement`, `material_included_list` and `author_statement_list`.
- **Search phrases** (code/fetch.py): "AI-generated", "artificial intelligence", "generated by AI", "AI-assisted",
  "generative AI", Midjourney, ChatGPT, Suno, Udio, "Stable Diffusion", DALL-E. "AI generated" without the hyphen
  returns the same 5,019 records as "AI-generated". "Claude" (27,862) and "Gemini" (6,975) were not searched: they are
  mostly personal names and the zodiac sign.
- The server is slow (0.5 to 12 seconds a record); every request is retried up to six times.

## What counts
- A registration counts only if an AI phrase appears in one of the applicant's own authorship statements: material
  excluded, material included, or an author's authorship statement. Titles, names, notes and rights statements are
  ignored, so books *about* AI and companies called "AI Something" drop out.
- The AI phrase is `AI` as a word, "artificial intelligence", "machine-generated", or a tool name (Midjourney, ChatGPT,
  GPT-n, Suno, Udio, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, Leonardo, Firefly, Sora, Runway, ElevenLabs, Claude, Gemini, Copilot,
  "LLM", "large language model"). The bare word "generative" is not enough (human generative art exists).
- **What the AI made** is read from the pieces of the excluded statement that carry an AI phrase (split on commas,
  semicolons, full stops, "and", "&"): images, text, lyrics, music and sound, narration and voice, video, code. A
  registration can name several. "AI-generated material" or "content" with no part named is "not said".
- **Books** = type of work "Text"; **songs** = "Music", "Sound Recording and Music", "Sound Recording".
- **Hedged disclaimers**: excluded statements with "(if any)", "may be/may have been … AI", "possibly", "potentially",
  "to the extent".

## Denominator and the lag
- Total registrations per month and type of work come from the same API: a wildcard query (`*`) restricted to one
  month's registration dates returns the catalogue's full histogram (code/denominator.py). Each month runs from the 1st
  00:00:00 to its last day 23:59:59 (an earlier version ended on the next month's 1st and counted those records twice).
- Records reach the catalogue weeks or months after the registration date. A month is "complete" if it holds at least
  80% of 2025's median month. The chart and the table stop at the last complete quarter.

## Checks
- Field-level filter: of the records the keyword search returned, the share dropped because AI appeared only in a
  title, name or note is in `summary.json` (`dropped_title_only`).
- Two subsets compared before writing: records found by any phrase other than "artificial intelligence", and a random
  sample of the records found only by "artificial intelligence". The books and songs splits agreed within a few points.
- Hand check: 36 records read (12 books, 12 songs, 12 music) against the automatic parts; one false "code + music" was
  found ("photos from different sources. Some material may be generated using AI": "sources" and "using" matched) and
  the patterns were fixed ("source code", word-start "sing", split on full stops).
- Organisations are named; private individuals are counted, never named. The full records (claimant street addresses,
  authors' birth years) are not in this repository; `data/registrations.csv` keeps only registration number, date,
  type, class, claimant name, countries, the statements and the title.

## Limits
- It counts disclosure, not use. People who used AI and did not say so are not here.
- Phrases outside the list ("computer-assisted", tools not named above) are missed.
- A tool name can match an ordinary word: at least one record is kept only because of a Hindi title, "Suno Bhai
  Sadho" ("suno" = "listen"). Records kept only through a tool name, with no "AI", "artificial intelligence" or
  "generated" in the statements, were 53 at the partial count; 15 were read, and all but that one are real ("Text edited with
  ChatGPT, all artwork created with Dall-E").
- The Office's own published figure (Law360 headline, April 2026: 6,000) is lower than this count for the same period;
  the Office has not published its method.
